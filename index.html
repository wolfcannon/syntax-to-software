22 steps and two level-ups

From Syntax to Software

  • Starts at what a statement is. Ends with a tested project you could hand to someone else.
  • Every idea is shown working on real data before you are asked to write it.
  • No programming experience, and no maths beyond arithmetic.

Most courses stop once the language is covered, this one keeps going: the last unit is version control, tests, two real bug reports worked end to end, and a layout you could clone.

How it works

  • Each unit builds on the last, starting at the most basic constructs of Python
  • Incrementally build a project to answer a statistical question using real data
  • Learn to set up an environment to run your own code as a natural part of the lesson progression
  • Interactive questions and exercises are included with each lesson

The question

GivenA walkable neighbourhood is one where destinations are close together and the streets connect.

GivenHow someone travels to work depends partly on how easy each way is.

SupposeMore walkable neighbourhoods have more people cycling to work.

Two public datasets can test that, because both describe the same unit of geography: a census block group, a few hundred households.

  • EPA National Walkability Index — scores every block group in the country from 1 to 20
  • Census Bureau commute survey — counts how people in each block group get to work

Join them on the block group and the question becomes arithmetic.

The course tests it on Marion County, Indiana — 632 block groups — and spends as much time on the places that break the pattern as on the pattern. A neighbourhood that scores 18 and cycles nothing is telling you something the correlation is not.

Each step advances that project. By step 3 you are building census block group identifiers, by step 8 working out why 12 of them refuse to join, and by the capstone you are reporting a correlation alongside how much of it could be noise.

The data

Both datasets are in data/. Nothing to download.

SourceWhat it providesRows
EPA National Walkability Index Walkability score 1–20 plus its four ranked inputs 632 block groups
Census ACS 5-year, table B08301 Commuters by mode of transportation 630 block groups
PeopleForBikes City Ratings Bike network quality, 0–100 (capstone only) by city

They do not line up. 7 block groups have a walkability score and no commute data, 5 have commute data and no score, 14 have no workers at all, and 9 carry the census "estimate unavailable" sentinel -666666666. You meet all four in Unit 1.

One honest caveat

The walkability data is real. The commute data is synthetic — shaped like the real thing and plausibly related to the real scores, but generated. Units 1–3 are unaffected; the capstone's findings are not meaningful until it is replaced. README.md has the three commands.
